# Morra — business brief

## What Morra is

Morra Market Services Limited is a WhatsApp-first assisted-shopping service for Lagos. It helps customers access selected goods from Balogun Market without spending a full day navigating traffic, heat, crowds, haggling, and cash-heavy market errands.

Morra is not currently a general delivery app or a full e-commerce marketplace. The pilot is a human-supported buying service: customers browse what is available in WhatsApp or send a specific request, Morra sources and checks it, and the customer either collects it from the CMS hub or uses Morra’s delivery partner for a disclosed area-based fee.

The name comes from the Yoruba phrase “Mo ra” — “I bought.” It captures the small, satisfying win of leaving the market with exactly what you came for at the right price.

## The customer problem

Balogun has useful products and strong market prices, but getting there can be difficult. Customers may need to deal with:

- Traffic and heat on the way to the market.
- Crowds, touts, pressure, and prices that feel inconsistent.
- A full day lost to moving through the market.
- The risk and inconvenience of carrying cash in a busy environment.

Morra removes the trip while keeping the local market connection.

## The offer

Morra lets a customer:

1. Browse the current WhatsApp catalog or send a market list.
2. Request a single item or a wholesale quantity within the pilot categories.
3. Confirm availability, the item price, fees, and the preferred pickup or delivery option.
4. Pay the one-time ₦500 order-validation fee so Morra can send a runner to source the order.
5. Let Morra source the item from Balogun and check it at the CMS hub.
6. Pay by transfer or card before arriving, or at the CMS counter when collecting; delivery customers complete payment before delivery.
7. Pick up for free at CMS or receive the order through the delivery partner during the operating window.

The customer gets Balogun access with less friction, a clearer buying experience, and a choice between free pickup and paid home delivery.

## Initial categories

- Fabric by the yard.
- Sealed cosmetics and skincare.
- Perfumes.
- Jewelry and accessories.

The category promise is intentionally narrow at launch: start with goods Morra can source and check with confidence, then expand as the operating rhythm becomes reliable.

## Sourcing requests

Customers can come with what they want to buy at the market instead of waiting for a catalog listing. Morra can source individual items or wholesale quantities within the four pilot categories. If an item is outside the current categories, customers can still tell Morra about it; those requests help determine what to add next, but they are not part of the current sourcing promise.

## Pilot operations

- **Sourcing location:** Balogun Market.
- **Pickup hub:** CMS, Lagos Island.
- **Pickup days:** Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday.
- **Pickup hours:** 4:00–6:00 PM.
- **Free pickup:** Available at the CMS hub during the 4:00–6:00 PM window.
- **Partner delivery:** Available during the same 4:00–6:00 PM window for a fee based on the delivery area, capped at a few orders per day.
- **Delivery pricing:** The delivery fee is disclosed before the customer makes full payment.
- **Primary order channel:** WhatsApp.
- **WhatsApp number:** +234 701 717 4639.
- **Instagram:** [@morra.market](https://www.instagram.com/morra.market).
- **Facebook:** [Morra on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/share/19BbitYAcF/?mibextid=wwXIfr).

Every order goes into the next operating day. An order placed on Wednesday, for example, is prepared for Thursday’s 4:00–6:00 PM pickup or delivery window.

Pickup is free and remains the default pilot experience. Partner delivery is a paid option for customers who cannot make the CMS slot or prefer home delivery. The delivery fee must be shown before full payment.

## Trust promises

Morra’s trust position is practical and specific:

- The customer sees the item price and applicable delivery fee before full payment.
- Morra checks the item against the order at the CMS hub.
- Wrong or damaged goods are exchanged rather than passed on to the customer.
- Customers do not need to hand cash to a runner in the market.
- The real Balogun price, 5% service and packaging fee, and one-time ₦500 validation fee are clear.
- If an item sells out before the runner arrives, Morra sends a live photo of the closest alternative on WhatsApp; the customer decides whether to accept it.
- If the customer misses pickup, Morra confirms whether to arrange paid delivery or apply a small holding fee for extra days at the hub.

These promises should only appear in customer-facing copy if the team can consistently deliver them.

## Audience

The primary audience is Lagos residents and commuters who want Balogun products but value time, predictability, and convenience. The strongest early audience is likely to be working people and other customers whose normal route makes CMS pickup practical.

The audience is not looking for a complicated new app. It needs a familiar channel, a direct answer, visible availability, and a simple handoff.

## Positioning and voice

Morra should feel:

- Local without being hard to understand.
- Direct without being cold.
- Culturally fluent without forcing slang into every sentence.
- Practical, transparent, and slightly warm.
- Confident about the service while honest that this is a pilot.

Useful phrases from the current direction include “without the wahala,” “on your way home,” “no long talk,” and “one WhatsApp chat.” Use them selectively so the voice feels natural rather than performative.

Avoid positioning Morra as a broad delivery platform, a marketplace with live inventory, or a promise of instant fulfillment. The current truth is assisted sourcing plus scheduled pickup or limited partner delivery on market days.

## Landing-page conversion goal

The website has one job: move a qualified visitor into a WhatsApp conversation.

The primary CTA is **Chat on WhatsApp**. Every primary CTA should open the same number with a short prefilled message asking to see what is available or to share a sourcing request. Secondary links should explain how the service works, what can be ordered, how individual and wholesale requests work, when pickup or delivery happens, and why the customer can trust the process.

## Operational details to keep clear

The public site should consistently communicate these current rules:

1. A one-time ₦500 order-validation fee is paid upfront to confirm the order and send a runner. It is refunded if Morra cannot source the item and is non-refundable if the customer cancels.
2. Customers may pay by transfer or card before arriving, or at the CMS counter when collecting. Delivery customers complete payment before the partner delivery.
3. The 5% service and packaging fee is added to the item total. Any partner delivery fee is separate, based on the delivery area, and shown before full payment.
4. Orders are prepared for the next Tuesday, Thursday, or Saturday operating day.
5. CMS pickup is free from 4:00–6:00 PM. Partner deliveries run during the same window and are capped at a few orders per day.
6. A missed pickup can become a paid delivery or incur a small holding fee for each extra day at the hub.
7. The exact exchange window, holding-fee amount, delivery-area pricing, and delivery-partner identity should be supplied to customers in WhatsApp when needed.
